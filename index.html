What is DEXA

Dual‑Energy X‑ray Absorptiometry.

DEXA is a low-dose X-ray scan that measures what your body is made of — fat, lean tissue, and bone. Here's what the name means.

Dual Energy

The use of two different X-ray energy levels.

X-ray

A low dose of ionizing radiation.

Absorptiometry

A way to measure how much of the X-ray energy is absorbed by the body.

Two scans, one technology

What a DEXA scan can measure.

Bone Density

Measures bone mineral density to help assess bone health and evaluate for osteoporosis.

Body Composition

Estimates fat mass, lean mass, and body fat distribution.

Fat mass

Fat mass is the total weight of the body's fat tissue.

It includes:

  • Subcutaneous fat: fat stored just beneath the skin.
  • Visceral fat: fat stored deep in the abdomen around internal organs.
  • Fat stored in other areas throughout the body.

Why it matters: Fat plays important roles in energy storage, hormone function, insulation, and protecting organs. The amount and distribution of fat can also help assess certain health risks.

Lean mass

Lean mass is the body's non-fat soft tissue.

It includes:

  • Muscle tissue
  • Organs and other soft tissues
  • Body water
  • Connective tissue

Why it matters: Lean mass supports movement, strength, physical function, and many essential body processes. Monitoring changes can be especially useful during strength training, aging, and weight-management programs.

How DEXA separates the two

Three components, measured separately.

A DEXA body composition scan estimates three main components:

Fat mass

Total estimated fat tissue, typically reported in pounds or kilograms.

Lean soft tissue

Muscles, organs, water, and other soft tissues, excluding fat and bone mineral.

Bone mineral content

The mineral content measured in the skeleton.

Total body mass=Fat mass+Lean soft tissue+Bone mineral content

“Your body weight tells us how much you weigh, but it doesn't tell us what your weight is made of.”

A DEXA body composition scan helps us distinguish fat tissue from lean tissue and measure bone mineral content. This gives us a more detailed picture of your body composition than the number on a scale alone. Your weight tells you how much you weigh. A body composition scan gives you a deeper look at what makes up that weight.

Using advanced DEXA technology, a body composition scan estimates your body fat, lean mass, and fat distribution, including visceral fat in the abdominal region. It helps you better understand how your body is changing beyond what you see on a scale.

The goal

Understand more than your weight. See measurable changes in your body composition over time.

Body composition scans provide measurements and estimates, not a diagnosis of overall health or fitness.

What you'll learn

  • Body fat mass and body fat percentage
  • Lean mass, including muscle and other lean tissues
  • Fat distribution throughout the body
  • Visceral adipose tissue (VAT), an estimate of fat within the abdominal cavity

Who might benefit?

Individuals focused on fitness, strength, healthy aging, athletic performance, or monitoring body composition changes over time. It may also be useful for people working with a healthcare professional on weight-management goals, including those using GLP-1 medications.

Bone density in Camas

Includes radiologist report

Your bones support you every day, but changes in bone density often happen without noticeable symptoms.

A diagnostic bone density scan uses DEXA (dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry) technology to measure bone mineral density, helping a qualified healthcare provider assess bone health and evaluate for osteoporosis.

The goal

Gain important insight into your bone health so you and your healthcare provider can make informed decisions about your care.

Important: Diagnostic bone density testing is different from a general wellness scan. Results require appropriate clinical interpretation by a qualified healthcare provider.

What you'll learn

  • Bone mineral density measurements
  • T-scores and Z-scores, when applicable
  • Whether bone density falls within expected ranges for the appropriate comparison group
  • Information that helps your healthcare provider assess osteoporosis risk and determine appropriate follow-up
  • A radiologist report

Who might benefit?

People who have been referred for bone density testing, individuals with risk factors for osteoporosis, and those whose healthcare providers recommend screening or monitoring bone health.

Bone health facts

Know your numbers. Understand your body. Protect your future.

A DEXA scan provides valuable insight into your bone health and body composition. In a quick, painless scan, you can learn more about bone density, body fat, and lean muscle mass — information that can help you make more informed decisions about your health. DEXA is a quick, noninvasive test that is commonly used to measure bone mineral density, particularly at the hip and spine.

Your bones can lose strength without you knowing it.

Osteoporosis is often called a "silent" disease because bone loss can occur without obvious symptoms. A DEXA scan can identify low bone density before a fracture occurs and can help healthcare providers assess fracture risk and monitor bone health over time. According to the CDC, nearly 1 in 5 women and 1 in 20 men over age 50 are affected by osteoporosis.

Bone health is about more than age.

Bone density can be affected by age, genetics, body weight, certain medications, medical conditions, hormonal changes, and lifestyle factors. Understanding your baseline can give you valuable information to discuss with your healthcare provider. NIAMS notes that DEXA can help identify osteoporosis, detect low bone density before osteoporosis develops, predict fracture risk, and monitor treatment effectiveness.

Who can benefit

A DEXA scan may be worth considering if you're…

  • An adult concerned about bone health
  • Someone with a family history of osteoporosis
  • An individual who has experienced certain fractures
  • A postmenopausal woman
  • An older adult
  • Living with certain medical conditions or risk factors
  • Taking medications that may affect bone health
  • Interested in establishing a baseline for future comparison

Before you arrive

What to wear — keep it simple, keep it metal-free.

Best choices

  • Comfortable clothing with no metal — t-shirts, sweatshirts, athletic wear, leggings, or sweatpants
  • Elastic-waist pants
  • A simple sports bra, or a bra without metal hardware
  • Clothing that's easy to remove or change if needed

What to avoid

  • Zippers, metal buttons and snaps
  • Belts and belt buckles
  • Underwire bras
  • Clothing with metal decorations, rivets, or grommets
  • Metallic fibers
  • Jewelry, watches, and piercings
  • Clothing with objects in the pockets

DEXA uses very low-dose X-rays to distinguish between bone and soft tissue. Metal can appear very dense on the X-ray and may interfere with the measurements. Consistent clothing also improves comparability between future scans.
